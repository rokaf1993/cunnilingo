the c-word

The blunt word for the vulva in 82 languages, with what it actually costs to say it. The same syllable is a career-ending insult in one country and a greeting in another.

Three families, one idea

The pizda family

Russian, Polish, Czech, Serbo-Croatian, Lithuanian, Latvian, Albanian and, by borrowing, Romanian. All descend from Proto-Indo-European *písdeh₂, built from *h₁epi (upon) and *sed- (to sit): the thing one sits upon. Old Prussian peisda and Persian pīzī kept that sense and came out meaning arse.

The kunta family

English cunt goes back through Middle English cunte and queynte to Proto-Germanic *kuntǭ, with Faroese and Icelandic kunta and West Frisian kunte alongside it. Middle Dutch conte produced modern Dutch kont, which now just means backside.

The fitta family

Swedish and Norwegian fitta, Danish fisse, Finnish and Estonian vittu and vitt. The Norse origin is disputed and most likely runs back to Old Norse fuð. Finnish is not a Germanic language at all, which makes its membership a borrowing rather than an inheritance.

The outliers

Latin cunnus looks like the English word and may have nothing to do with it; the link has never been shown, and Partridge preferred cuneus, a wedge. Turkic am runs unbroken from Turkish to Uzbek. Hebrew kus shares its form with Arabic.
